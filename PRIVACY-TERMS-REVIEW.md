# Relay privacy and service-terms review checklist

This is a preparation checklist for the actual Relay operator and a qualified adviser to complete before real customer data or payment is accepted. It is not legal advice, a privacy notice, a data-processing agreement, or a customer contract. Do not publish unverified statements as commitments.

## Facts to confirm before editing the public pages

- [ ] Legal name and contact details of the person/entity providing Relay.
- [ ] Country/state where the operator is established and where customers will be served.
- [ ] Whether an adult, company, or other eligible party must operate accounts, sign contracts, or receive payments.
- [ ] Production host, database/storage region, backups, logs, reverse proxy, and support access.
- [ ] Email provider, inquiry-notification provider, and whether Ray/AI is enabled; identify the account configuration and relevant processing locations/terms.
- [ ] Product-data retention and deletion workflow, including backups and deletion replay after restore.
- [ ] Monitored support/privacy contact and how users can submit access, correction, export, deletion, or complaint requests.
- [ ] Final price, billing interval, payment method, taxes, renewal, cancellation, refunds, and any usage limits.
- [ ] Actual security/availability/support commitments. Do not promise controls or service levels that are not configured and tested.

## Privacy notice review

### Controller/operator and scope

- [ ] Identify the responsible legal operator and a monitored privacy contact.
- [ ] Explain which activities Relay controls (account, workspace, customer-form, inquiry, support, and service telemetry).
- [ ] Clarify the relationship between the SaaS customer/workspace owner and the end customer whose setup information is submitted. The operator may have different responsibilities for each data flow; obtain qualified advice.
- [ ] State the audience, availability, and effective date of the notice.

### Data categories and purpose

- [ ] Describe account email, password hash, sessions, memberships, invitations, recovery records, and appearance/onboarding preferences.
- [ ] Describe process maps, tasks, templates, customer names, due dates, blockers, assignments, requirements, and submitted customer inputs.
- [ ] Describe inquiry/demo fields and explain that company/email details are stored within the inquiry message under current implementation.
- [ ] Describe technical logs, IP/proxy data, cookies/session identifiers, and any host-level telemetry that will actually be collected.
- [ ] Explain why each category is processed and avoid collecting unrelated, sensitive, authentication, payment-card, or confidential material in customer fields.

### Sharing and processors

- [ ] Name or accurately categorize the real hosting, email, inquiry-notification, and AI providers.
- [ ] Describe the data each provider receives and why. In particular, Ray receives the user's question and recent conversation only after consent; do not imply that AI-provider handling is governed solely by Relay.
- [ ] Confirm provider retention, training/use settings, region, subprocessors, and contractual terms before making claims.
- [ ] Explain any cross-border transfers and safeguards only after confirming the actual deployment and applicable law.

### Retention, rights, and security

- [ ] State retention periods for live account/workspace/inquiry data, application logs, provider records, and backups. Match actual configuration; distinguish a policy target from implemented enforcement.
- [ ] Explain account deletion: personal account data, sole-member workspace deletion, member removal, required shared-workspace ownership transfer, and data remaining in backup snapshots until expiry.
- [ ] Describe how an older backup is prevented from restoring deleted accounts/workspaces or undoing ownership transfers.
- [ ] Provide a workable route for access, correction, export, deletion, objection, or complaints as applicable. Do not promise a feature that does not exist.
- [ ] State security controls at a truthful level and avoid claims of certification, penetration testing, encryption, or availability unless evidenced.
- [ ] Explain breach/incident communications based on the actual operator process and applicable requirements; do not invent deadlines.
- [ ] Confirm cookie/session behavior, analytics/tracking (if any), age eligibility, and how minors are handled.
- [ ] Have the complete notice reviewed against the actual jurisdiction, customer contracts, provider settings, and deployment.

## Service terms / order form review

- [ ] Identify the provider and contracting party, supported jurisdiction, eligibility/authority to contract, and governing law after review.
- [ ] Define account eligibility, credentials, team member responsibilities, workspace ownership, acceptable use, and suspension/termination process.
- [ ] Define service scope and boundaries: Relay does not synchronize with external project systems or configure customer infrastructure; readiness signals are not certification; Ray may be inaccurate and does not perform product actions.
- [ ] Define customer responsibilities for lawful use, permission to submit data, choosing safe customer-input fields, protecting invitation/customer links, and removing access when no longer needed.
- [ ] State data ownership, permitted processing, support access, subprocessors, confidentiality, data-return/deletion process, and any required data-processing/security addendum.
- [ ] Specify supported features, plan limits, availability/maintenance expectations, support channel/hours/response target, and whether any SLA applies.
- [ ] Specify price, currency, billing interval, payment provider, taxes, invoicing, renewal, cancellation, refunds, trials, usage limits, and overdue-payment handling.
- [ ] Address intellectual property, feedback, warranties/disclaimers, liability limitations, indemnity, force majeure, dispute process, termination, and post-termination data handling with qualified review.
- [ ] Ensure the quote/order and public pricing page agree; no quote should omit the amount, period, limits, and cancellation/refund terms.
- [ ] Have the terms and order reviewed by a qualified adviser familiar with the actual operator's age, jurisdiction, and B2B SaaS obligations.
