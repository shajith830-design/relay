# Relay: 10-minute product demo

## Recording setup

- Audience: prospective customers and newly onboarded client teams.
- Format: 16:9 screen recording, 1080p, browser zoom 100%, cursor movement slow.
- Use a clean browser profile with no personal tabs, notifications, or saved credentials visible.
- Use the public sample workspace at `https://relayautomation.tech/product.html?demo=workspace`.
- The sample workspace uses fictional data and browser-local changes. Say so clearly; do not imply this is a live customer account.
- Do not show the owner console, real customer information, passwords, access tokens, or private email.
- Speak at a calm pace. Let the screen breathe during the marked pauses. The timecodes include narration, clicks, and pauses and total 10:00.

## Timed script and shot list

### 0:00–0:40 — Opening

**On screen:** Start on the Relay homepage. Keep the hero in view. Fade in the title: “Relay — clearer customer implementations.”

**Narration:**
“Welcome to Relay. In the next ten minutes, I’ll show how a SaaS team can map its customer onboarding process, turn that process into a reusable template, and track the work and information needed to move each implementation forward. We’ll also look at Ray, Relay’s built-in guide, and finish with how a client gets access after choosing a plan. This walkthrough uses Relay’s public sample workspace, so all customer and team records you’ll see are fictional.”

### 0:40–1:25 — The customer implementation problem

**On screen:** Briefly scroll the homepage through its feature cards, then return to the hero.

**Narration:**
“Customer onboarding often involves several people, a sequence of setup steps, and information that has to come from the customer. When that work is spread across messages and separate trackers, it can be difficult to tell what is complete, what is waiting, and what needs attention. Relay brings the process, customer-specific tasks, and requested setup information into one workspace. It is designed as a standalone workspace rather than a connector to external project-management tools.”

### 1:25–2:05 — Plans and a guided demo

**On screen:** Open Pricing. Show Starter, Growth, Custom, and the comparison table. Do not rush the prices.

**Narration:**
“Relay offers three plan options. Starter is 2,999 rupees per month for up to three team members, five active implementations, and three templates. Growth is 3,999 rupees per month for up to ten team members, twenty active implementations, and unlimited templates. Growth also includes prerequisites, blocker tracking with next actions, and template revision history. Custom capacity and pricing are agreed in advance. Prices are before applicable taxes. If a team wants a walkthrough tailored to its process, it can request a guided demo from the website.”

### 2:05–2:35 — Enter the sample workspace

**On screen:** Navigate to `https://relayautomation.tech/product.html?demo=workspace`. Wait for the sample workspace to load. Keep the sample-data banner visible.

**Narration:**
“Now I’m opening the public sample workspace. Notice the banner: this is fictional sample data, and changes stay in this browser. It is not connected to a Relay account or backend. That makes this a safe place to explore the workflow before a team starts using its own workspace.”

### 2:35–3:25 — Overview and what needs attention

**On screen:** Show Overview and the sample implementation row or summary. Point to progress and readiness indicators without making up values that are not on screen.

**Narration:**
“The overview is the starting point for the team. It gives you a place to review customer implementations and see where follow-up may be needed. Rather than treating onboarding as a single status, Relay brings together the tasks and requested information that contribute to readiness. The goal is to help the implementation owner identify the next action and keep the customer journey moving.”

### 3:25–4:20 — Map the process

**On screen:** Select Process map. Open “Customer onboarding” and show its Kickoff, Configuration, and Launch steps. Point to instructions, roles, and assigned members.

**Narration:**
“A consistent implementation starts with a clear process. In Process map, the team can describe the steps, organize them into stages, add instructions, identify the responsible role, assign a teammate, and set a due-date offset. Here the sample process moves from confirming project goals, to configuring the workspace, to reviewing launch readiness. The sample also shows prerequisites, so a later task can depend on earlier work. Before using a process repeatedly, the team can test it and check that it is ready to save.”

### 4:20–5:00 — Reuse a tested template

**On screen:** Select Templates. Show “Standard onboarding.” Highlight that it is based on a saved process; avoid editing sample content.

**Narration:**
“Once a process is checked, the team can save it as a reusable template. Templates help reduce the need to rebuild the same workflow for every customer. The team can start from a known structure, then manage the work for each customer as its own implementation. Growth and Custom plans also include template revision history and restore, which helps teams review changes to their reusable workflows.”

### 5:00–6:20 — Follow one implementation

**On screen:** Select Implementations. Open “Acme Example Co.” Show the task list and statuses. Point out the in-progress configuration task and the launch task waiting for its prerequisite, if visible.

**Narration:**
“Here is a sample customer implementation created from the reusable template. Each implementation has its own tasks, owners, statuses, and due dates. The team can review what is complete, what is in progress, and what remains. On plans that include them, prerequisites make task order visible, and blocker tracking lets a team record why work is blocked and its next action. These details help turn a general process into work the team can coordinate for one customer. Everything here is sample data; a real client workspace is private to that client’s team.”

### 6:20–7:15 — Request customer information

**On screen:** In the implementation, scroll to the customer-information section. Show the required project contact email and optional website field. If showing the customer link, use only the demo link and clearly label it as a sample.

**Narration:**
“Some implementation steps depend on information only the customer can provide. Relay lets the team define requests such as a project contact email or a website address, including whether a field is required and what kind of answer it expects. The implementation can share a private customer link for submitting those details. Relay can then surface required information that is still missing, so the team knows what to follow up on. For this recording, I’m only showing fictional sample information—never enter a real customer’s private details into a public demo.”

### 7:15–8:05 — Ask Ray

**On screen:** Open Ask Ray. Ask: “How do I start a customer implementation from a template?” Show the answer. Then ask: “What does a prerequisite do?”

**Narration:**
“Ray is Relay’s built-in guide for questions about supported Relay workflows. For example, I can ask how to start an implementation from a template, or what a prerequisite does. Ray uses a curated library of Relay-specific answers; it is not a general-purpose AI assistant and does not connect to an external AI service. If a question is outside the workflows Ray supports, it should say so rather than pretend it can help.”

### 8:05–9:10 — How a paid client gets started

**On screen:** Return to the pricing page, then show the public Contact or Book a Demo page. Do not open or reveal the private owner console.

**Narration:**
“For a new client, the public website is where they review plans and contact Relay or request a guided demo. Relay does not take payment or create a paid workspace through public self-signup. After the plan and payment are confirmed with the client, the Relay owner creates their workspace and account. The client receives their sign-in details through a secure channel and can then invite teammates from inside the workspace. The owner-only setup page and its access token are never shared with clients.”

### 9:10–10:00 — Close and next step

**On screen:** Return to the homepage. End on the Relay logo and “Book a Demo” / “Contact” links.

**Narration:**
“To recap: Relay helps teams map a repeatable customer implementation process, save it as a template, coordinate customer-specific tasks, request the information needed for setup, and see where follow-up may be required. Ray can guide users through supported Relay workflows. The public sample workspace is available to explore, while a paid client’s real workspace is provisioned separately after the plan is agreed. If you’d like to see how Relay fits your team’s onboarding process, use the Book a Demo or Contact link on relayautomation.tech. Thanks for watching.”

**End card:** “Relay Automation” · “Customer implementation readiness” · `relayautomation.tech`
